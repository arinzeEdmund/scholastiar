# Grants

Status: Unified Platform Service

## Feature Vision

Scholastiar.ai Grants is a future AI-powered grant application system that helps individuals, researchers, startups, NGOs, student founders, social impact projects, creators, small businesses, and organizations discover, prepare, apply for, and manage grants globally.

It should reuse the same core intelligence as Scholarships:

> The user explains themselves once. Scholastiar.ai helps them apply everywhere intelligently.

But Grants should go deeper into project strategy, budget building, impact measurement, funder alignment, compliance, and post-award reporting.

Every grant opportunity should also be evaluated for its cross-border value: whether it can fund international research, relocation, market entry, global project delivery, travel, expansion into another country, or a stronger pathway toward greener pastures for the applicant, team, or community.

The platform should not only help people find grants. It should help them turn a real idea, project, organization, or research plan into a fundable application package.

Example user intents:

> I run a youth education nonprofit in Nigeria and need grants for digital skills training.

> I am building a climate-tech startup and need early-stage innovation grants.

> I need research funding for a public health project.

The platform should help users find relevant grants, check eligibility, shape the project narrative, generate tailored answers, build a budget, prepare documents, apply through assisted forms or hosted applications, track deadlines, and prepare reports after funding.

The emotional promise:

> You do not need to understand the entire grant world alone.

## Problem Being Solved

Grant applications are painful because applicants often struggle with:

- finding legitimate grants
- understanding complex eligibility criteria
- matching their project to the right funder
- translating an idea into funder language
- writing strong project summaries
- building realistic budgets
- defining measurable outcomes
- explaining impact clearly
- preparing logic models or theories of change
- writing monitoring and evaluation plans
- submitting organizational documents
- proving credibility
- managing deadlines
- answering long repetitive forms
- adapting the same project to different funders
- avoiding scams and fake grant opportunities
- tracking submissions manually
- preparing post-award reports if funded

Many good projects fail because the applicant cannot express the idea in the format funders expect.

Scholastiar.ai Grants should reduce this friction and help users build credible, truthful, funder-aligned applications.

- What outcomes do you expect?

- How will the project be sustained?
- What is your budget justification?
- What risks might affect the project?

Each mapped answer should show:

- original question
- AI draft answer
- project facts used
- missing information
- risk or confidence level
- edit field
- approval checkbox

### Grant Scam And Legitimacy Protection

The platform should flag risky grants.

Risk signals:

- unofficial payment requests
- no funder history
- suspicious email domains
- unrealistic funding promises
- no application criteria
- copied text from other sites
- fake foundation behavior
- requirement to pay before applying
- no verifiable official website

Verification statuses:

- verified
- source checked
- unverified
- risky
- reported

## Risks And Constraints

Key risks:

- inaccurate grant data
- outdated deadlines
- fake grants
- websites blocking iframe embedding
- browser restrictions preventing direct autofill
- terms-of-service conflicts on external sites
- AI-generated proposals sounding generic
- AI hallucinating project facts
- users inventing budgets or impact numbers
- privacy risk around financial and organization documents
- overpromising funding success
- operational complexity of human reviews
- post-award compliance complexity

Important constraints:

- AI must never fabricate organizational credibility, finances, or impact
- users must review and approve final answers and proposals
- platform should distinguish assisted external forms from platform-hosted submissions
- auto-fill should only happen where technically and legally allowed
- grant verification status must be visible
- success probability should be framed responsibly
- budget and financial data require strict access control
- post-award reporting tools must not generate false evidence
